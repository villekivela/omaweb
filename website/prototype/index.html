Keyboard-driven browser

The browser should be part of your desktop, not a guest on it.

Tanto takes its palette from your terminal, its geometry from your window manager, and its commands from your keyboard. One ordinary window, tabs down the side, and browsing identities that never see each other.

2 keys

Nothing needs the mouse

Addresses, tabs, Spaces and every browser command live behind one overlay — ⌘K. Links get letter hints with f. Scrolling is j and k, because of course it is.

Your bindings, not ours

Every keystroke is a line in one JSON file you own. Rebind anything, hand a conflicting key back to the page, and read the live sheet whenever you forget one.

Themed from your terminal

Point Tanto at a terminal colour scheme and the whole browser follows — chrome, Start page, and the inspector docked beside your tab.

3 spaces

One browser, several people

A Space owns its logins, cookies, history, permissions, sessions and tabs. Switch away and the first Space is simply not there — no profile juggling, no second browser, no signing out of your own work to check a client's dashboard.

Pinned tabs that stay put

Pin the handful of pages a Space is actually about and they come back every time it resumes. Mark one Keep active and it keeps running while you are elsewhere — and Tanto always tells you which tab that is and what it is costing you.

4 state

What works today

Spaces, Pinned tabs, the Omnibar, keyboard navigation, docked developer tools, content blocking, session restore, downloads, history and site permissions — on macOS, in one frameless window.

What is not finished

Certificate handling, sandbox health reporting, URL reputation, updates and signed packages. Tanto is pre-alpha and says so. Do not put anything sensitive through it yet.

Where it is going

The same browser contract on Linux and Wayland, sitting in a tiling window manager as a first-class citizen rather than a 200MB tenant.

Why it is built this way

A browser is where the working day happens. It should behave like the rest of the tools that day is made of.

One window

Tabs run down the side where their titles fit. No tab strip that shrinks every page you open into an unreadable sliver.

Separate identities

A Space keeps its own logins, cookies, history and permissions. Your work and a client's dashboard stop having opinions about each other.

Your theme, everywhere

Import a terminal colour scheme and the chrome, the Start page and the docked inspector all follow it. No extension, no userChrome hack.

    Nothing needs the mouse

    Every command is bound, and every binding is a line in one JSON file you own.

    One overlay for everything

    Addresses, search, tabs, Spaces and every browser command live behind Ctrl K. Links get letter hints with f. Scrolling is j and k, because of course it is.

    Your bindings, not ours

    Rebind anything, hand a conflicting key back to the page, and read the live sheet whenever you forget one. The Start page above is that sheet.

    Themed with the rest of the desktop

    Run omarchy theme set and the browser changes along with your bar, terminal and editor. Tanto watches the theme Omarchy generates — no hook to install, no second colour scheme to maintain.

    Two accounts, one window

    A Space is an isolated profile, not a different person. Personal and work, side by side.

    The same site, signed in twice

    A Space keeps its own cookies, logins, history, permissions and session. Open your own account in one and your work account in another and the site never sees them as the same visitor — no second browser, no private window, no signing out to switch.

    Pinned tabs that stay put

    Pin the handful of pages a Space is actually about and they come back every time it resumes. Mark one Keep active and it keeps running while you are elsewhere — and Tanto always tells you which tab that is and what it is costing you.

    Where it stands

    Tanto is pre-alpha and says so. Do not put anything sensitive through it yet.

    Working now

    Spaces, Pinned tabs, the Omnibar, keyboard navigation, docked developer tools, content blocking, session restore, downloads, Space-local history and site permissions.

    Not finished

    Certificate handling, download verification, sandbox health reporting, URL reputation, updates and signed packages. All of it is required before anyone browses anything that matters.

    Linux is coming

    The complete browser contract under native Wayland on Omarchy and Hyprland, with the packaging to install it the ordinary way. A browser that belongs in a tiling window manager instead of merely surviving one.
